# Assumptions and provisional decisions

These decisions optimize for a near-zero-budget, sell-first pilot. They are
reversible and must be revisited when usage, regulation or a paid contract changes
the constraints.

| ID      | Decision/default                                                                                                      | Rationale                                                                                              | Revisit trigger                                                                             |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| DEC-001 | Use a TypeScript modular monolith with Fastify, grammY, PostgreSQL, Drizzle, Zod, Pino and Vitest.                    | One runtime, strict contracts, small dependency/operational footprint and easy future web reuse.       | Team capability evidence or CP-01 prototype shows a material disadvantage.                  |
| DEC-002 | Start as one package and one deployable process, while enforcing domain/application/adapter boundaries.               | Avoid premature workspace and deployment complexity.                                                   | A second deployable product or independent release cadence exists.                          |
| DEC-003 | Use separate resident and staff bot tokens in the same process.                                                       | Smaller privilege surface without paying for another host.                                             | Independent scaling, security ownership or release cadence is needed.                       |
| DEC-004 | Use long polling for demonstration and the first low-volume pilot.                                                    | No domain, TLS termination or public ingress cost; simple local-to-pilot parity.                       | Multiple bot replicas, strict inbound integration, or meaningful traffic requires webhooks. |
| DEC-005 | Use PostgreSQL locally and in the pilot.                                                                              | Transactions, unique idempotency keys, concurrency control and future reporting justify it.            | No planned revisit.                                                                         |
| DEC-006 | Do not add Redis or a broker; poll a transactional PostgreSQL outbox.                                                 | Reliable notifications without another service.                                                        | Outbox throughput or isolation measurements justify extraction.                             |
| DEC-007 | Use `Asia/Tashkent` for business display/reporting and UTC storage.                                                   | Environment and business location align.                                                               | MCK operates across business timezones.                                                     |
| DEC-008 | Pilot languages are Uzbek Latin and Uzbek Cyrillic; translations live in resources.                                   | Fits the source and likely resident scripts without adding a third language.                           | Buyer requires Russian or usage research changes priorities.                                |
| DEC-009 | Pilot one mahalla, four service categories and four combined roles.                                                   | Smallest scope that demonstrates the complete operating loop.                                          | Paid expansion.                                                                             |
| DEC-010 | Commercial pricing, contract and payment are conditional records and feature-flagged out of the first sale.           | The presentation includes them, but they are not required to prove request execution value.            | Customer funds or requires a commercial workflow.                                           |
| DEC-011 | Priority is a versioned five-factor weighted model; feasibility is displayed separately.                              | Preserves explainability and prevents expensive safety work being labelled unimportant.                | Operational evidence supports recalibration.                                                |
| DEC-012 | Potential duplicates are suggested; only authorized staff may link them.                                              | Automatic merging can erase legitimate resident demand and provenance.                                 | High-quality labelled data supports safer automation.                                       |
| DEC-013 | Pilot attachments are JPEG/PNG photos up to 10 MB, represented by Telegram file metadata/IDs.                         | Avoid object-storage cost while keeping the first workflow useful. They are not contractual archives.  | Evidence must be retained independently or documents/videos are enabled.                    |
| DEC-014 | One administrator identity is bootstrapped securely; other staff are pre-approved and linked explicitly.              | Telegram usernames and callback visibility are insufficient identity proof.                            | An organization identity provider is available.                                             |
| DEC-015 | Store closed-request PII for 12 months and ordinary photos for 90 days, then anonymize/delete unless a case is open.  | Data minimization default pending policy review.                                                       | Legal/business retention decision overrides it before production.                           |
| DEC-016 | Internal pilot targets are configurable and not shown as contractual resident promises.                               | MCK capacity and legal SLAs are unverified.                                                            | Service owner approves external commitments.                                                |
| DEC-017 | Keep request validation and order execution as separate lifecycles linked one-request-to-one-order initially.         | Prevents rejected intake from becoming work and keeps operational reporting unambiguous.               | Validated consolidation rules require many requests per order.                              |
| DEC-018 | Role grants are global only when explicitly unscoped; normal operator/executor grants are scoped to a service area.   | Provides least privilege without adding a policy engine or paid identity platform.                     | Multi-organization rules require tenant-aware policy evaluation.                            |
| DEC-019 | Order transition writes use optimistic versions and append history/audit in one PostgreSQL transaction.               | Prevents lost updates and partial audit records using the existing zero-cost database.                 | Measured contention requires a different concurrency approach.                              |
| DEC-020 | Store each Telegram update result and serialize per-resident intake in PostgreSQL.                                    | Provides retry/concurrency safety without Redis, a queue, or another paid service.                     | Sustained throughput measurements justify a separate ingestion queue.                       |
| DEC-021 | Use a database sequence for `MCK-YYYY-NNNNNNNN` request tickets.                                                      | Human-readable, collision-free tickets without exposing personal or Telegram identifiers.              | Multi-region writers or buyer-mandated numbering rules require another allocator.           |
| DEC-022 | Derive overdue from persisted deadlines and create an idempotent PostgreSQL escalation in an automated scan.          | Meets deadline visibility without another scheduler, queue, or paid hosting service.                   | Approved business calendars or measured scan load require a dedicated scheduler.            |
| DEC-023 | Store controlled Telegram before/after evidence metadata without downloading the media.                               | Provides a usable first-sale execution record with no object-storage cost.                             | Contractual evidence, retention independence, or malware scanning becomes required.         |
| DEC-024 | Allow either an owner-linked resident or scoped operator to accept/request rework unless a category is operator-only. | Meets the unresolved acceptance question with a safe configurable default.                             | Operations owner approves a different category rule.                                        |
| DEC-025 | Use a seven-day operational warranty, 24-hour rework target, and 48-hour complaint-review target in the pilot seed.   | Provides a demonstrable closed loop without claiming contractual service levels.                       | Buyer/operations/legal approves category-specific contractual targets.                      |
| DEC-026 | A complaint records a case but never reopens an order automatically; scoped staff must reopen with a reason.          | Prevents accidental lifecycle changes while keeping resident complaints visible.                       | Approved automation retains an explicit accountable decision.                               |
| DEC-027 | Electrical work requires a passing checklist inspection; the other seed categories allow optional inspection.         | Demonstrates enforceable risk-based configuration without adding friction to every low-risk service.   | Pilot evidence or owner policy changes the category classification.                         |
| DEC-028 | Deliver bounded notification templates at-least-once through a PostgreSQL outbox with five bounded retry attempts.    | Prevents lost material updates and exposes failure without Redis, a broker, or arbitrary PII payloads. | Measured volume, multi-channel delivery, or duplicate intolerance justifies extraction.     |
| DEC-029 | Run reminders and alert scans in the application process with a PostgreSQL advisory lock.                             | Adds no hosting component and remains safe if a second process is introduced.                          | Jobs need independent scaling, strict timing, or a buyer-managed scheduler.                 |
| DEC-030 | Compute live weekly/month-to-date reports in PostgreSQL using Tashkent boundaries and documented formulas.            | Delivers a sellable summary and CSV without BI, warehouse, or snapshot infrastructure.                 | Paid immutable period close, high query latency, or multi-timezone operation.               |
| DEC-031 | Keep profitability out of operational reporting until revenue, cost, tax, and allocation ownership are approved.      | Prevents invented financial claims and belongs with CP-09 commercial definitions.                      | Financial source-of-truth and accounting formulas are approved.                             |
| DEC-032 | Use explicit, audited PDCA actions with a strict lifecycle; do not auto-create actions from free text.                | Gives managers accountability without unreliable AI classification or another service.                 | Sufficient labelled evidence and approved automation policy exist.                          |

## Constraints

- No secrets are committed or requested in chat.
- Demonstrations use synthetic data until privacy text is approved.
- No claim of legal, accounting or security certification is made.
- Application code begins only after CP-00 is approved.
